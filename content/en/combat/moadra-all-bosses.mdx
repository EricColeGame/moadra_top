## The Pre-Boss Checklist That Saves Runs

Most boss deaths in metroidvanias are not skill failures; they're preparation failures. Before you walk through a fog gate, run this list.

| Check | Why It Matters | Time Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Spend your currency | Dying with a full wallet is the worst outcome in the genre | 1 minute |
| Confirm your movement upgrades | Some bosses are near-impossible without a specific traversal tool | 2 minutes |
| Top off healing | Entering a fight at half health is a self-inflicted handicap | 1 minute |
| Scout the arena | Identify walls, pits, and hazards before the fight starts | 2 minutes |
| Set your loadout intentionally | Ranged options and mobility tools solve different fights | 1 minute |
| Do one reconnaissance attempt | Watch patterns instead of trying to win | 3 minutes |

That reconnaissance run is the single highest-value habit on the list. Go in planning to lose, and spend the whole fight watching the boss's animation start-up frames rather than its health bar. Players who do this typically cut their total attempts on a given boss by a wide margin — the information you gather is worth more than the damage you'd have dealt.

## Reading a Fight Phase by Phase

Nearly every modern boss fight is structured in thirds, and the tactics that work in phase one will get you killed in phase three. Build the habit of re-evaluating at each health threshold.

| Phase | What Usually Changes | Your Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Opening (100–70%) | Boss establishes a small move pool | Learn the two or three most common attacks; stay patient |
| Escalation (70–40%) | New move added, speed increases | Stop fishing for extra hits; punish only hard recoveries |
| Desperation (40–0%) | Arena hazards activate or the boss goes berserk | Play defensively, take guaranteed openings only, protect your healing |

The biggest mistake in the desperation phase is greed. When a boss enters its final third, players instinctively try to end the fight fast — which is exactly when the boss's damage output peaks. Slower, cleaner play wins more of those fights than aggression does.
