## Story and Setting: Redemption on Protea VI

You play as Moadra, a lone figure traversing the sand-swept planet Protea VI, roughly 20,000 years after your ancestors went extinct. The pitch is blunt: endure, evolve, or die. You are hunting redemption, and the thing standing between you and it is a "terrifying sin" buried deep in the forgotten planet.

Gloomsoft leans hard into dread. The store description name-drops desolate surfaces, freezing summits, and a research facility crawling with hostile alien species. The developers also flag mature content directly: Moadra contains frequent violence and gore. This is not a cozy exploration game with a spooky coat of paint.

## Combat, Movement, and the Evolution Loop

### Movement is the whole pitch

Moadra's movement kit reads like a greatest-hits list for the genre: roll, dodge, leap, wall jump, and sprint. Gloomsoft markets the sprint speed with a "never before seen" boast, which is marketing language, but the design intent underneath is clear. Traversal is a skill check, not a formality. Expect to die to a gap before you die to a boss.

### Combat rewards reflexes over stats

Fights mix an arsenal of weapons with special abilities, and the difficulty curve runs from nuisance creatures to gargantuan monsters that demand every tool you have. The "Difficult" tag is not decoration. Community reports and the tag cloud both point to a game that expects you to learn enemy patterns rather than out-level them.

### Protoplasm is the engine

The progression hook is simple and satisfying. Destroy creatures, absorb their Protoplasm, then spend it inside organic chambers to trigger metamorphosis. That process permanently evolves your abilities and weapons.

| System | How it works | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Movement set | Roll, dodge, wall jump, sprint | Skill-based evasion and traversal |
| Weapons | Arsenal expanded and upgraded over time | Answers to different enemy types |
| Special abilities | Unlocked through progression | Needed for giant encounters |
| Protoplasm | Dropped by defeated organisms | The core upgrade currency |
| Metamorphosis chambers | Consume Protoplasm inside organic rooms | Permanent ability and weapon evolution |
| Scanner bot | X-ray vision and mapping | Reveals hidden paths and secrets |

## Exploration, Secrets, and the Scanner Bot

Protea VI is described as a massive, interconnected world, and the nonlinear structure is one of the game's biggest selling points. You are encouraged to poke at the map at your own pace rather than follow a corridor of set pieces. Secrets, hidden routes, and optional paths are scattered throughout.

The scanner bot is the smartest design decision in the package. Activate it and you get X-ray vision that exposes creatures and terrain detail invisible to the naked eye, reveals hidden paths, and maps the areas you have explored. In a genre where players routinely burn hours humping walls for secrets, a built-in discovery tool is a genuine quality-of-life win.

This part of the Moadra review comes with one caveat. Nonlinear design cuts both ways: players who need a clear next objective may find the opening hours meandering. The scanner helps, but it does not hold your hand.

Both. It is built on an interconnected, ability-gated 2D map, but combat leans on an arsenal of weapons and special abilities rather than melee alone. Steam's own tags list Metroidvania, Shooter, Platformer, and Action side by side.

Yes. It holds Steam Deck Verified status, and Gloomsoft pushed a pre-launch demo update aimed at handheld players. The 9 GB install size and low minimum specs also help.

**How long is Moadra?**
