## Moadra Impressions: What Gloomsoft's Alien Metroidvania Gets Right

The pitch is simple and bleak. You play as Moadra, a lone survivor picking through the ruins of Protea VI, a planet whose civilization collapsed 20,000 years ago. Your goal is to uncover your lost Neera heritage while confronting what the developers coyly call "the terrifying sin" buried beneath the surface. It is a story-forward setup delivered through environmental storytelling rather than cutscene dumps, and it gives the exploration a genuine pull.

What sells the world is the tonal commitment. Protea VI is not a colorful playground with spooky wallpaper — it is a hostile, sand-swept place where the surface bakes, the summits freeze, and the research facilities are overrun with things that want you dead. The art direction leans into dark sci-fi horror, and the atmosphere is consistently cited by early players as the game's strongest asset.

The structure is a massive interconnected map rather than discrete levels, which is exactly what genre fans want to hear. Hidden paths, optional secrets, and shortcuts are everywhere, and the game expects you to pay attention to geometry you cannot yet reach. That "come back later" design is the backbone of any good Metroidvania, and Moadra leans on it hard.

## Combat, Movement, and Difficulty — The Make-or-Break Layer

Movement is where the game immediately separates itself from slower, more deliberate genre entries. Moadra rolls, dodges, leaps, wall jumps, and sprints at a pace that feels closer to a character-action game than a traditional explorer. Chaining a wall jump into a sprint into a dodge is smooth, and the speed is genuinely useful — not just a traversal gimmick — because the planet is full of hazards that punish standing still.

Combat mixes that mobility with a small arsenal: melee and ranged options, special abilities on cooldown, and encounters that scale from nuisance fauna to gargantuan monsters that demand you use every tool you have. The design intent is clearly "read the tells, use your whole kit," and the difficulty tag on the store page is not marketing decoration.

| Tool | Function | Impression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roll and dodge | Short-range evasion with i-frames | Essential; forgiving enough to learn, tight enough to master |
| Sprint | High-speed traversal and repositioning | Makes backtracking painless |
| Wall jump | Vertical routing and secret access | Opens the map in satisfying ways |
| Weapons and abilities | Damage output and utility | Encourages swapping rather than maining one option |
| Scanner bot | X-ray vision for creatures, paths, and mapping | The single best quality-of-life feature in the game |

That scanner bot deserves its own paragraph. Activating X-ray vision lets you inspect creatures, reveal paths invisible to the naked eye, and map out where you have been. In a game this dense, a tool that respects your time without removing the discovery is a smart piece of design — and it is the reason Moadra's backtracking rarely feels like busywork.

The caveat: difficulty spikes are real. Community reports describe certain encounters as walls that require you to leave, upgrade, and return. If you bounce off hard games quickly, this one will test your patience before it rewards it.

## Protoplasm, Metamorphosis, and the Progression Loop

Most Metroidvanias gate progress behind keys and abilities found in fixed locations. Moadra adds a resource layer on top: destroy hostile organisms to absorb Protoplasm, a biomass currency, then spend it inside organic chambers to trigger metamorphosis. That process upgrades your abilities and weapons to new tiers.

It is a clever twist because it ties progression to the fiction. You are not collecting coins; you are literally consuming the planet's ecosystem to become something that can survive it. It also creates a risk-reward rhythm — the creatures you need to farm are the same creatures that can kill you.

| System | What it does | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protoplasm | Dropped by defeated organisms | The core upgrade currency |
| Organic chambers | Locations where Protoplasm is spent | Creates natural return trips and checkpoint rhythm |
| Metamorphosis | Upgrades abilities and weapons | Ties character growth to the world's biology |
| Scanner mapping | Reveals hidden paths and secret areas | Reduces aimless wandering without removing mystery |
| Nonlinear routing | Multiple viable exploration orders | Rewards curiosity and replays |

The combination of a nonlinear map and a spendable resource means two players can hit the same boss with noticeably different kits. That flexibility is a big part of why the game's "difficult" tag has not translated into a wall of negative reviews — there is usually another route, another upgrade, or another tool to try.

## FAQ

They draw on the launch build's documented systems, the updated public demo, and early player reports from the October 22, 2026 release. Because the review sample is still small, treat long-term judgments as provisional.

The store tags it as difficult, and community reports back that up. The Protoplasm and metamorphosis systems give you outs — you can usually upgrade or reroute instead of bashing your head against one encounter — but the game does not hold your hand.
