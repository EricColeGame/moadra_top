## What Actually Goes Into a Moadra Tier List

If you want to rank Moadra yourself — or sanity-check someone else's ranking — you need weighted criteria. These are the six that matter most in this genre.

| Criterion | Weight | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Movement and combat feel | 25% | Does controlling the character feel good moment to moment? |
| Map and exploration design | 25% | Are secrets earned or stumbled into? Is backtracking purposeful? |
| Progression hook | 20% | Does the upgrade path feel meaningful, not padded? |
| Boss and enemy design | 15% | Are fights readable, fair, and memorable? |
| Art, audio, atmosphere | 10% | Does the world sell itself? |
| Performance and polish | 5% | Does it run cleanly and respect your time? |

A few practical tips for applying this framework:

- **Score movement first.** In a Metroidvania, you spend more time moving than fighting. Clunky traversal caps a game at B Tier no matter how good the art is.
- **Track your backtracking mood.** If you dread re-crossing a zone, exploration design is failing. If you look forward to it, the map is doing real work.
- **Separate "hard" from "unfair."** Difficulty is a feature; unreadable hitboxes are a bug.
- **Note the first hour and the tenth hour.** Games that open strong and fade usually land in B. Games that grow land in A.
- **Weigh your own genre history.** If this is your first Metroidvania, everything feels fresh — adjust for that.

For context on how the genre's conventions evolved and why these criteria are weighted the way they are, the [Wikipedia entry on Metroidvania design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metroidvania) is a solid primer on the map-gating structure that separates the greats from the also-rans.
