## Why the Moadra Metacritic Score Is Still Blank

A game that launched yesterday is almost never fully scored. Three things typically have to happen before a Metascore appears.

**Critics need time to finish.** Metroidvanias are long. A reviewer who wants to comment on map interconnectivity, ability gating, and late-game boss design usually needs a full playthrough, not a weekend. Outlets that received code late will publish late.

**Metacritic waits for a minimum sample.** Metacritic generally holds back a Metascore until a handful of critic reviews are in — historically around four for most games — so that one outlier outlet can't define the aggregate. Until that threshold is met, the score reads as pending.

**User reviews open after a short delay.** Metacritic has moved to a post-launch window for user submissions on many titles, which is why the Moadra Metacritic user section still says reviews aren't available. It's a moderation decision, not a signal about quality.

## How Metacritic Turns Moadra Reviews Into a Score

Understanding the math makes the waiting less maddening. Metacritic converts each critic's score to a 0–100 scale, then weights outlets — larger, more established publications carry more influence than a small blog. That weighted average is the Metascore.

User scores live in a separate bucket on a 0–10 scale, and they are not folded into the critic number. This is why a game can show a strong Metascore and a weak user score at the same time, or the reverse.

| Metascore band | Metacritic's label | What it usually means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100 | Universal acclaim | Genre-defining; rare for a debut Metroidvania |
| 75–89 | Generally favorable | Solid, recommendable, with clear flaws noted |
| 50–74 | Mixed or average | Divisive design choices; read individual reviews |
| 20–49 | Generally unfavorable | Structural problems across most outlets |
| 0–19 | Overwhelming dislike | Near-universal rejection |

For a first release from a small studio, the 75–89 band is the realistic target zone, and the 50–74 band is where "interesting but rough" Metroidvanias tend to land. Anything in the high 80s would be an unusually strong result for a launch with no established reputation behind it.

## Tracking the Moadra Metacritic Page Without Getting Misled

The biggest mistake players make is treating a two-hour-old score as final. Here's a cleaner way to follow the Moadra Metacritic listing over its first month.

- **Check the review count, not just the number.** A 78 built on six reviews is a rumor; a 78 built on forty is a verdict.
- **Read the outliers.** One 40/100 among mostly 80s usually explains a specific complaint — often performance or difficulty spikes.
- **Separate critic and user scores.** They measure different things and often diverge on niche genres.
- **Cross-check platform-specific performance.** A Metascore blends all platforms; a Switch-specific frame rate problem can hide inside a good average.
- **Ignore the percentages until you have real ratings.** Zero ratings produce zero information.
