## The Three Pillars: Endure, Evolve, or Die

The tagline is not marketing fluff — it describes three loops you will cycle through constantly. Understanding them turns confusion into a plan.

| Pillar | What It Means in Play | Beginner Action |
|---|---|---|
| Endure | Survive long enough to learn enemy patterns and hazard timing | Slow down; observe one new threat before engaging it |
| Evolve | Gain new abilities that unlock routes and change how you fight | Prioritize traversal upgrades over raw damage early on |
| Die | Failure is part of the design, not a verdict on your skill | Treat each death as information about what to change |

### Exploration discipline

In any Metroidvania worth its map, the fastest route forward is often the one you cannot take yet. When you hit a ledge that is just out of reach or a door that will not open, mark it mentally or with the in-game map tools and move on. New players who fixate on a single locked path burn an hour on a wall the game expects them to leave alone until later.

### Combat patience

The trailer emphasizes bizarre creatures and deadly hazards, which usually means telegraph-heavy encounters rather than damage races. Approach a new enemy type with a defensive first attempt: dodge, watch, and note the wind-up animation. The second attempt is where you actually fight back. That two-step habit will save you more health than any upgrade.

## Your First Hour: A Moadra Beginner Guide Checklist

The opening stretch of a Metroidvania sets your entire pacing. Work through this list before you start chasing story beats.

| Step | Priority | Why It Pays Off |
|---|---|---|
| Check the control and accessibility settings | High | Remapping or adjusting assists early prevents frustration later |
| Test every starting ability in a safe area | High | Knowing exact jump and attack ranges stops accidental deaths |
| Learn the map legend and marker system | High | Fewer wasted backtracking trips |
| Identify what a checkpoint looks like | High | Determines how boldly you can explore |
| Fight one new enemy type at a time | Medium | Isolates patterns instead of creating chaos |
| Note every locked path you pass | Medium | Turns dead ends into a future route list |

If the game offers any tutorial prompt about evolving or upgrading, do not skip it. In most Metroidvanias, the first ability you gain defines the next two hours of routing, and misunderstanding it can leave you wandering a zone you were never meant to clear yet.

## Common Beginner Mistakes (and Better Habits)

Every guide repeats "explore thoroughly" and "be patient," which is not actionable. These pairings are.

| Mistake | Better Habit | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Sprinting through new rooms | Clear rooms at walking pace the first time | You will spot breakable walls, hidden ledges, and ambush positions |
| Hoarding upgrades for later | Spend early upgrades on survivability and mobility | Longer survival means more exploration per attempt |
| Rushing a boss on the first try | Use attempt one to learn tells, not to win | Pattern knowledge converts directly into damage windows |
| Ignoring the map | Mark locked routes and suspicious dead ends | Backtracking becomes a checklist instead of guesswork |
| Treating death as failure | Note what killed you before retrying | Repeated deaths usually share one fixable cause |

If you want a single habit from this Moadra beginner guide to carry forward, make it the last one. Death is written into the game's tagline for a reason — it is a teaching tool, not a penalty screen to rage through.
